What is “the testimony of Jesus” in Revelation 19:10?

▶ Watch this part (0:00)

Short answer: He says the angel told John not to worship him but God, because the angel and the brethren “have the testimony of Jesus,” and that testimony “is the spirit of prophecy.” He says this angel had been speaking by the inspiration of the Spirit for 19 chapters.

▶ 0:00 We’re going to start in Revelation chapter 19:10. Remember, the angel has been talking to John for 19 chapters, and, uh, it’s the same angel that’s in Revelation 17:7. The angel said to me, it’s the same one talking. And, uh, John fell at his feet to worship this angel, and the angel said, “Don’t do that. Worship God. I am your fellow servant and of your brethren. I am the servant of the same master as you and your brethren.” See, worship God, and me, the angel, the fellow servant, and the brethren have the testimony of Jesus. What do they have? The testimony of Jesus. So there is a testimony of Jesus, and then he tells us what that testimony is: the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy.

What does “the spirit of prophecy” mean, and why does the church need it?

▶ Watch this part (1:13)

Short answer: He says prophecy “means speaking by the inspiration of the Spirit,” not human intellect, wishes or opinions, but “the Spirit’s truth and facts.” He says Jesus’s testimony, the evidence of who He is, is the spirit of prophecy, and the church needs the witness and proof of Jesus shown by it.

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 1:13 Prophecy, and that’s what this angel had been doing for 19 chapters. He had been speaking by the inspiration. Prophecy means speaking by the inspiration of the Spirit. The Spirit’s involved, not your human intellect, not your human wishes, your human opinions, but it’s the Spirit’s truth and facts that is being spoken. And so the testimony, the witness of Jesus, is the spirit of prophecy. Now, if you’ve ever been in a courtroom (I’ve been there several times), a person gives their testimony. They’re giving the testimony, the evidence of what they experience, or what they are, or who they are, or whatever, you see. So Jesus’s testimony, the evidence of who He is, is the spirit of prophecy. When His presence comes, the proof that it’s Jesus’s presence is the spirit of prophecy.

▶ 2:25 Are you out there? Now, I know this is new to a lot of people, but we have been religious for too long in America. We need to get into Bible doctrine and Bible experience. Is that an amen? Yes! We need to buck up and come up. So in the church, it is imperative that we have the witness, the proof, the evidence, the testimony of Jesus, and that would be shown by the spirit of prophecy. Hallelujah. See, Paul said in 1 Corinthians 14, “Desire spiritual gifts, but rather that you...” What? What? Prophesy: speak by the inspiration of the Holy Ghost. How many can say amen to that? You know, healing lasts for the time that you get healed, but tomorrow and the days after, in all your life, you’ve got to talk, and some of us don’t talk too well, do we? So we need that, the spirit of prophecy.

What boundaries keep prophecy safe? (2 Peter 1:19-21)

▶ Watch this part (5:42)

Short answer: He says Peter gave the boundary: a “more sure word of prophecy,” which is not of private interpretation and did not come “by the will of man.” He says Isaiah and Jeremiah were only allowed to speak what the Spirit told them, and “when the Spirit speaks, He don’t care what you believe.”

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 5:42 And so we need boundaries for this, don’t we? And here’s the boundaries. Peter said, “We have a more sure word of prophecy. No prophecy of the Scripture is of any private interpretation,” any personal understanding. See, it’s not human ability. He said, “But the prophecy came not in old time by the will of man.” See, men’s opinions, men’s thinking, men’s deciding of what’s right and wrong had nothing to do with this. It was totally the Spirit. Doesn’t that make you feel safe? Yeah. Isaiah and Jeremiah, they weren’t allowed to throw in their opinions. They were only allowed to write and speak what the Spirit told them to. They didn’t say, “I believe.” Where did Isaiah and Jeremiah say, “Well, I believe it’s this way”? Is it in there? No. The Psalms: does the psalmist ever say, “Well, I believe this is what is truth”? No. Why? The Spirit’s talking, and the Spirit is a Spirit of what? Truth. And when the Spirit speaks, He don’t care what you believe. And I can prove it to you.
