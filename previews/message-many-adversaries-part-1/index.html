Why did the early church meet on the first day of the week?

▶ Watch this part (0:00)

Short answer: He cites Luke 24:1, Acts 20:7 and Revelation 1:10 to show the disciples met on the first day of the week because Jesus rose from the grave on that day. He says history shows Roman reports that Christians “meet on the first day of the week,” though Acts also shows them in the synagogue on Saturday.

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 0:00 We are in chapter 16 of 1 Corinthians, in verses 1-4. He talks about collecting for the saints that are in Jerusalem on the first day of the week: take up the collection, so when I come there’s no collections to be taken up. And, uh, there’s a big argument in America and throughout the world on what day we should serve Jesus, what day we should come to church, Saturday or Sunday. In Luke 24:1 we find that on the first day of the week Jesus rose from the grave, on Easter Sunday, the first day of the week. So see, they met on Sunday, the first day of the week, because Jesus rose from the grave. We find in Acts 20:7 that on the first day of the week, when the disciples came together to break bread, Paul preached to them until one guy fell out of the window.

▶ 1:05 See, they say I’m a long-winded preacher here, but let me tell you, Paul has got me beat by a long shot. But anyway, they met together on the first day of the week. When you study history, you will find that Roman rulers, lower ranks in authority, would have to report to higher ranks, and they would say, “These Christians meet on the first day of the week, and they take communion, and they make a vow that they will not commit adultery and, and, uh, steal and, and do these bad things, on the first day of the week.” So there’s plenty of evidence that the early church met on the first day of the week. But also, if you read Acts, you’ll find them coming into the synagogue on Saturday also. They were there Saturday and Sunday if they could be. Revelations 1:10, John the apostle was in the Spirit on the Lord’s Day. Now, what’s the Lord’s Day? The first day of the week, Sunday. So do we meet together on Sunday, or do we meet together on Saturday? And let me tell you, there’s a big argument out there. I debated out at the college with a bunch of college students about this thing. They, they just get young Christians wrapped up in silly things like Saturday or Sunday. It’s just a, a silly thing.

▶ Watch this part (2:24)

You know, your shadow isn’t the real thing. It brings you to the real thing. And so the Sabbath day was just an example of something God wanted to teach us.

▶ 3:10 All right, let’s, in Acts chapter 1:3, after Jesus rose from the grave, He was seen of them 40 days. Was He only seen of them on Sunday? Was He only seen of them on Saturday? No. He was seen of them anytime that they would accept seeing Him: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, and Saturday. Acts 1:14, the 120 people in the upper room continued 10 days in prayer. The Bible says in one place. So did they leave on Monday? Did they leave on Tuesday? Would God turn His back on them if they met on Monday? No. Oh, come on, church. And so it’s important that you understand that whether we meet on Saturday, Sunday, Monday, Tuesday or Wednesday, God accepts you coming together and seeking Him. Can I hear an amen? And we need to get rid of these silly little things that we argue and fight about and get on the real thing.
