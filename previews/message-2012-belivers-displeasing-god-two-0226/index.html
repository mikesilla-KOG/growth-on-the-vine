What does it mean to tempt (test) God? (1 Corinthians 10:9)

▶ Watch this part (0:00)

Short answer: He says “tempt means to test,” and that Israel tested God by asking whether He could give them meat as well as water and manna. He says God can do anything, but “does he always say yes to your prayers? No.”

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 0:00 Verse 9: “Nor let us tempt God,” test Christ, “as some of them also tempted and were destroyed by serpents.” See, he again uses Christ. You notice, recognizing that Rock that was there giving them that water. “Tempt” means to test. And what they did is they said, “Well, God gave us this water and He gave us manna, but can He give us meat? Can He spread a table of meat in the wilderness?” Well, they had sheep and oxen, they had all kinds of animals around there, but the thing is, can God do this? If God is really that big. And so what do you do? That’s a testing God. Of course He can. God can do what? Anything. But does He want to do it? Not always. Does He always say yes to your prayers? No. When your prayers are dumb, He’s going to say no.

Why does God sometimes say no to prayer, and what does the Bible say about complaining? (1 Corinthians 10:10)

▶ Watch this part (0:59)

Short answer: He says “God is a good parent” who knows when to say no, sometimes because the timing is not right, like a child who asks for a bicycle he cannot yet ride. Reading verse 10, “nor complain,” he says the American church is good at complaining, “we complain when it’s cold, we complain it’s hot.”

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 0:59 I don’t care what these faith people preach. God is a good parent. He knows when to say no, not even because your prayer is dumb, but the timing is not right. Do you give your kid a bicycle till it’s the right time for them to have the ability to ride it? No. Even though they want one, two years old: “I want a bike, 24-inch bike, man, I want.” They can’t do anything with it now. The kid isn’t doing anything wrong, but he’s asking for something that he can’t utilize. There’s a time for everything. Amen. What does 1 Corinthians 10:13 mean by “temptation”? (the tire illustration)

▶ Watch this part (4:29)

Short answer: He says the word “temptation” means “an experiment to prove, a test to prove,” and compares it to tires tested for 10,000, 20,000 or 60,000 miles. He says God tests us “to show where we’re at” so we go to the next level, and that “I don’t know what Bible you’ve been reading” if you say nothing bad happens to Christians who have faith.

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 4:29 But then this great verse in verse 13. This is the verse I use all the time when I’m witnessing, uh, to establish people that say, “I can’t live for God.” This is a verse I use, 1 Corinthians 10:13: “No temptation has overtaken you except such as common to man.” Now, the word “temptation” means an experiment to prove, a test to prove. And you know, when they sell you a tire for your car, you can buy a tire that will last 10,000 miles and, you know, no road hazard protection, that’s all the tire is good for. Then they got them 40,000 and 60,000 miles. Why?

▶ 5:32 And what they do is they take them in the factory and they bend and flex it and beat them and drop them from an airplane and everything else, and get them on the raceway, and they drive them high speed, low speed, stop and starts and all that, to know and see, they proved that this tire will go 50,000 miles. Now, in our baby stage we’re good for 10,000 miles. That’s about one inch, say. And God comes to test us, what? To show where we’re at, so we learn from it and go to the next level. And boy, we think, “Man, hallelujah, man, I got it.” And then here comes another test. Now, is He trying to destroy you, trying to beat you up, trying to discourage you?
