Why does God compare his people to a filly among Pharaoh’s horses? (Song of Solomon 1:9)

▶ Watch this part (0:00)

Short answer: He explains that Pharaoh’s horses were trained to pull the king’s chariot and to go to war, and “they never ran from adversity.” He says God likens us to them, so many of his people “are scared to death of a little problem,” but we are “new creatures” being fashioned to “lead the king down to the streets.”

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 0:00 Before we get into the topic of restoration, I want to show you in the Bible one of the things that God compares his people to. Song of Solomon 1:9, “I have compared you, my love, to a filly among Pharaoh’s horses.” Now, Pharaoh would go out and he would get horses. Horses ran around in the wild, and they learned to tame them and they learned to train them. And a chariot back in those days was like an automobile today, okay, and also they used them in war. It was like a tank in war. And so these horses were trained to pull the king in his golden chariot, and they were trained to go to war, and in war they would go in there with swords clashing, arrows flying all around, all the noise, all the racket, people dying, they’d get hit by arrows, they’d be wounded, and these horses would just keep doing what they’re supposed to do.

▶ 1:13 They never ran from adversity, they never ran from being afraid of the enemy. Are you out there, church? Warfare was no problem to them, and when there was no war going on, they had no problem pulling the king, pulling the king, Alleluia, making a way for the king. And that’s what God likens us to here. And so many of God’s people are scared to death of a little problem. They’re scared to death of warfare. They’re scared to death of any kind of opposition, any kind of challenge or adversity in their life, and they shouldn’t be. We were born again, new creatures, old things are passed away. It doesn’t matter what we were, we are new people today that God is fashioning into a horse that can lead in battle and lead the king down to the streets where the people bow to the king.

▶ 2:23 Don’t you want to be that kind of church where you bring the presence of the king and people bow before the king? And when war comes, you’re not afraid of war.

Why is being in the king’s barn better than being independent?

▶ Watch this part (2:34)

Short answer: He says wild horses were affected by parasites, disease and famine, while those in the king’s barn were groomed, medicated and fed. He says “being independent isn’t always the best way,” and the church, likened “as a female,” should submit to “the king’s bridle.”

▶ 2:34 Now, he would take these wild horses. When you’re a horse is wild, see, they can do what they want to do, and that’s what a lot of Christians are. They just still want to do what they want to do. But when you’re out in the wild and no one to take care of these horses, we’re affected by parasites: the fly, the tick, the flea, some other chickers, and these things would carry diseases, and horses would die from disease. But see, when you were brought into the king’s barn, the king had groomers to take care of those things, and these guys were kept from the parasites, and there was also inner parasites that they would drink water or whatever and get these little microorganisms in them and it would kill them. ▶ 3:35 Out in the wild, when famine coming along and there was no rain and so forth and the springs dried up and there was no food to eat, the horses in Pharaoh’s barn ate well, they had plenty of water, but the independent horses, they starved, they got bony, they couldn’t find water. So see, being independent isn’t always the best way, is it? Hallelujah. And in Pharaoh’s barn he took care of them with medicine when they were sick. What is restoration, and why do even “good” people need it?

▶ Watch this part (5:04)

Short answer: He says restoration “just simply means to remake a wreck like new,” and that God wants to make “that wreck better than new.” He says even good people need it because “there is none good but God,” and compares it to a car that needs fixing and cleaning “every once in a while.”

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 5:04 Restoration just simply means to remake a wreck like new. You know, you take an old car, and Mr. Barnes just had his car detailed and it looks like it just came off the assembly line, but also when he has bad brakes he has to fix it and so forth, he has to keep the repairs up. And when people don’t keep the repairs up, the car winds up being a piece of junk, it’s in the junkyard, and then somebody that likes that car will go and get it, the 1957 Chevrolet or something, you know, they would get and they would restore it back to like new. That’s what restoration is. And see, we, through our own independent decision and doing things, we destroy our life, but God wants to restore it. He wants to make that wreck better than new.

▶ 5:59 This is the promise, this is what he wants to do. Now, why do all people need restoration, even good people? People say, “Well, I’m good, I don’t, you know, I don’t need restored.” But the Bible says that none, there is none good but God. Jesus said that when they called him good, he said, “Hold on, why do you call me good? There’s none good but the Father.” The God is the good one. And Jesus was God, but if he would have took recognition above God, then he would have sinned, and he can’t sin. Hallelujah. So us good people, what need restore? Mr. Barnes’s truck is not a wreck, but it needs fixing every once in a while. Amen. It needs cleaning.
