In this message Pastor David G. Kincer teaches that the baptism of the Holy Ghost with power is a real experience separate from salvation and water baptism. He starts with John 7:38-39 and Jesus as the baptizer, covers prophecy, visions and the names of the Holy Spirit, then walks through the accounts in Acts of Pentecost, the believers in Samaria, Cornelius’s household and the disciples at Ephesus. He also speaks about unity in worship, mistakes in Spirit-filled churches, and the purpose of the Spirit’s power.

What is the baptism of the Holy Ghost, and what does John 7:38-39 promise?

▶ Watch this part (0:00)

Short answer: He says there is “an experience in God called the baptism of the Holy Ghost with power,” not just something at Pentecost, at salvation or at water baptism. He reads John 7:38-39 as a promise to believers, and says the criteria for having the Holy Ghost is “Believe. Okay, it’s not complicated.” The Spirit flows from “your belly, innermost being,” and “it’s not a ceremony, it’s not a ritual, it’s a real experience.”

Who baptizes in the Holy Ghost, what is the power for, and what does it mean to be a witness? (Luke 3:16; 24:49; Acts 1:8; 22:15)

▶ Watch this part (3:14)

Short answer: He says “Jesus is the baptizer in the Holy Ghost,” who “shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost and with fire,” which is “what happened on the day of Pentecost.” He says “we are clothed with power when the Holy Ghost comes upon us,” and that the Holy Ghost coming upon you means “we put it on like a coat.” He adds that “a witness is someone who has seen or experienced facts,” and “we are proof that His salvation works.”

▶ 3:14 We find that Jesus is the baptizer in the Holy Ghost. All right, Luke 3:16: “I indeed baptize you with water, but One mightier than I comes,” this is John the Baptist talking, “the laces of whose shoes I am not worthy to unloose. He shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost and with fire.” And that’s what happened on the day of Pentecost, tongues of fire, baptism of the Holy Ghost. What is the difference between prophesying and predicting, and what kind of visions does he want? (Acts 21:9; 1 Corinthians 14:3)

▶ Watch this part (7:48)

Short answer: He says Philip’s daughters prophesied as “they spoke by the inspiration of the Spirit,” and “there’s a big difference when you go into predicting” and claiming the office of a prophet: “if you miss one time, the Bible says you’re a false prophet.” He says “prophesying for edification, exhortation, and comfort is totally different.” On visions, he wants the kind Peter and Stephen had, “not pizza nightmares,” and of the pizza kind he says “there’s no message in it, there’s no edification in it.”
