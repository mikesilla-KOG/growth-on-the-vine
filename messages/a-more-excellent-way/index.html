A More Excellent Way: full transcript and answers

Single message · Full sermon · Pastor David G. Kincer, Vine Cluster Church · Uploaded June 18, 2014 (preached March 25, 2012) · 29:19

Watch the full message on YouTube

In “A More Excellent Way,” preached March 25, 2012, Pastor David G. Kincer teaches from 1 Corinthians 12:31–13:13 that love is the more excellent way to the spiritual manifestations. Without love, he says, tongues, prophecy, knowledge, faith, giving and even martyrdom are worth nothing. He defines love from 1 Corinthians 13, says everyone in the church has a part and must grow up out of childish things, and says the goal of the commandment is love out of a pure heart.

What does “a more excellent way” mean in 1 Corinthians 12–13?

▶ Watch this part (0:00)

Short answer: He says Paul ends 1 Corinthians 12 by saying he will show a more excellent, more superior way to get spiritual manifestations, and that way is love. Without love, he says, tongues of men and angels are like a clanging cymbal, and prophecy, knowledge and all faith without love leave you “nothing.” He gives the man of God in 1 Kings 13 and Solomon as people who had the message or the wisdom but not the love.

Transcript of this part

▶ 0:00 And today we’re going to look at this subject, love, the more excellent way to spiritual manifestations. Remember, in 1 Corinthians 12:31, Paul ended that chapter by saying, “Desire the best gifts, and I will show you a more excellent, more superior way to get spiritual manifestations.” And then in 1 Corinthians 13:1 he says, “Though, or if, I speak with tongues of men and angels, but have not love, I am become a sounding brass or a clanging cymbal.” And if I had someone over there just clang the cymbals together, you know, in about thirty seconds you would go, “You’re driving me nuts. What’s the point?” See, cymbals without anything around them are worthless. And he says that’s people that speak with tongues of men. He describes two kinds of languages here. Tongues is nothing but languages. There’s languages of men, like they spoke on the day of Pentecost, and there are languages of angels. But the key is, love is more important than both of those.

▶ 1:12 In verse two, he says, “If I have prophecy, understand all mysteries, all knowledge, and have all faith, so I could remove mountains, but have not love, I am nothing.” I am worthless. I am zero.

▶ 1:27 Now, remember in 1 Kings 13, when the man of God was chosen by God to take a message to the king of Israel, Jeroboam, that God was going to disrespect the bones of the very leaders who chose to worship God out of his method? What did Jesus mean when He said “I never knew you”?

▶ Watch this part (2:42)

Short answer: He reads Matthew 7:22, where many say “Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in your name,” and says they had faith and the spiritual manifestations were working in them. Jesus still said, “I never knew you,” and he says, “You might have been used of God to do things, but you missed the important thing, and that is love.” He adds that Jesus used moving a mountain as a metaphor, and Paul says that even if you could and do not have love, “you are zero.”

Mary came in and she anointed Jesus’s feet with alabaster perfume, and Judas said, “Oh man, this stuff could be sold and it could be given to the poor.” The Bible even says there he didn’t care about the poor. What does love look like in 1 Corinthians 13:4–7?

▶ Watch this part (6:24)

Short answer: He walks through Paul’s definition: love is patient, kind and mannerly, does not envy, brag or behave rudely, and is not easily provoked. He says “love doesn’t demand its own way, in the family, in the church, or in the community. It demands the right way, the truth.” He says love bears all things, believes what the Bible says to trust, hopes that God will perform what he said, and remains, staying in the home and steadfast in the church.

Transcript of this part

▶ 6:24 And then he gives the definition of love. 1 Corinthians 13:4: Love is patient. It suffers long. Love is kind. It’s mannerly. Love doesn’t envy. It’s not jealous. Love doesn’t brag about itself. Love is not prideful, not puffed up. Love does not behave rudely, not ill-mannered. Love does not seek its own way. Well, it’s going to be my way, I’m going to hit the highway. You know, love doesn’t demand its own way, in the family, in the church, or in the community. It demands the right way, the truth.

▶ 7:03 Love is not easily provoked. It, you know, they’re not touchy. You know, if you don’t say the right thing or do the right thing, they’re all ticked off. And love doesn’t do that. It thinks no evil about people, no harm. It keeps no record of wrongs done to it. One version says, you see, love does not rejoice in iniquity, but in the truth. It’s not happy when the community goes wrong. It’s happy when the community goes right, or the church goes the right direction.

▶ 7:33 And check this out in verse 7. Love bears all things, which means covers with silence all things said to it. Pause for heart checking. Believes all things that the Bible says to put your trust in. They put their trust in all things the Bible says to. They hope; they expect that God would perform everything that he said he would perform. Not what people want, but what God wants. And love bears, or remains, in all things. No matter how it’s treated, no matter how mistreatment or whatever, it remains. It stays in the home and it stays steadfast in the church. It sticks right there. It doesn’t move.

Can you have the gifts of the Spirit without the fruit of the Spirit?

▶ Watch this part (8:22)

He says, “you can’t have the manifestations of the Spirit without the fruit and the divine character of God,” and that this is the way to get it.

Transcript of this part

▶ 8:22 And remember, love is at the first fruit of the gifts of the fruit of the Spirit: love, joy, peace. It’s the first fruit. 1 John 4:8 says, “God is love.” See, that is exactly what God is. We add to our faith virtue; virtue, knowledge; knowledge, temperance, or self-control; patience, godliness, brotherly kindness; and the last one we add to is what? Love. Have tongues and prophecy ceased?

▶ Watch this part (9:29)

Short answer: He reads that love never fails but prophecies fail, tongues cease and knowledge vanishes away, and says those are the same Greek word. He tells of Baptist leaders who said tongues and prophecies ceased when the last disciple died, and says he answered that the same verse says knowledge ceases, so if one goes you cannot take out two without the third. He also says that if prophecy ceased, Jesus would not be coming back, since that was prophesied.

Transcript of this part

▶ 9:29 Now he goes on to say, “Love never fails.” It never gets inactive. But in verse eight, he says if prophecies, they will fail; they will get inactive. If tongues, they will cease; they will get inactive. If knowledge, it will vanish away; it will be inactive. All of those three red statements up there is the same word in the Greek, just translated a little differently, but the same word.

▶ 9:59 So, I have been in Pentecost ever since I was saved. Spirit-filled church, Spirit-filled person. I got Spirit-filled a couple of weeks after I got saved. I’ve worked with the Baptists and other churches. God taught me at the beginning not to be segregated. I’ve always worked with other churches and did things with other churches. I was working with some Baptist people for several years here in California, and they invited me one day into the room with the top leader of California. The Southern California leader was there, the local leaders were there, and then the new preachers coming up through the ranks were there. They brought me in, and they said, “We have never met a Spirit-filled person like you.” My reply was, “How many have you met?” I said, “You are judging us because of what you see on TV, these guys whoosh, and everybody falling over and knocking over in the Spirit and all this. You’re judging the whole group of us by what you see on TV or hear on radio or something like that. If you got to know more of us, you’d know we’re not like that.”

▶ 11:10 And so then they quoted this scripture: “We believe that tongues and prophecies ceased when the last disciple died.” And they’re just quoting the verse. I said, “Sir, you didn’t finish the verse. Knowledge is there. And it says knowledge ceased. Now, if knowledge has ceased, not just tongues and prophecy, then that means people can’t come to the knowledge of salvation. People can’t have the knowledge of God, the knowledge of Christ, the knowledge of sin.” Are you with me? And they said, “Well, no, no, not knowledge.” I said, “You can’t take two out and do something with them without taking a third out.”

▶ 11:46 “And number two, if prophecies really cease, then Jesus isn’t coming back again, because it was prophesied in the New Testament that he would return.” I said, “It’s also prophesied that we come back for a glorious church without spot or wrinkle or any such thing. So that can’t happen. It’s also prophesied in Revelation that every kindred, tribe, tongue and nation, a multitude that no man can number, will be brought in. That can’t happen.” Are you there? Should we stop using spiritual gifts because people misuse them?

▶ Watch this part (12:26)

Short answer: He says that when the Baptist leaders said Pentecostals have made a mess of tongues and prophecy, he answered, “Just because a plane crashes does not mean we stop using planes.” He says Paul is talking about edifying the body of Christ, such as a prophecy, a tongue and an interpretation one Sunday, and not about the office of a prophet or the unchangeable words of God.

Transcript of this part

▶ 12:26 I said, “You’ve gotten rid of tongues and you’ve gotten rid of prophecy because you’ve seen the mess that us Pentecostals have done with it.” And I said this famous statement: “Just because a plane crashes does not mean we stop using planes. Just because someone messes up a salvation verse or some other verse, we don’t stop using the verses, do we?” Amen.

▶ 12:50 Truly, he’s talking about edifying the body of Christ. You see, we can have a prophecy one Sunday, or a word, a tongue, an interpretation, and we forget it the next day. Isn’t that true? Transcript of this part
