Division Concluded Part 1: full transcript and answers

The Corinthians series · Division Concluded, Part 1 · Pastor David G. Kincer, Vine Cluster Church · Uploaded June 18, 2014 · 22:50

Watch the full message on YouTube

In “Division Concluded Part 1,” Pastor David G. Kincer finishes the section of 1 Corinthians (chapter 4) where Paul deals with division. He contrasts how the Corinthians saw themselves with how real ministers lived, and lists Paul’s sufferings. Paul answers insults with good words, works with his own hands and asks for unity even when picked on. He says Paul wants fathers rather than mere talkers, and that the goal of the gospel is Christ formed in you.

What was wrong in the Corinthian church?

▶ Watch this part (0:00)

Short answer: He says Paul spent the first four chapters of the letter on division, warning the Corinthians about their proud, arrogant thinking. They were saying “I’m of Paul, I’m of Apollos, I’m of Peter,” and only one fourth had it right: “we’re of Jesus.” He adds that they were quarreling, jealous, carnal, and still “on the milk” after several years.

Transcript of this part

▶ 0:00 This is the words of Paul the Apostle. He got saved about five years after Christ died on the cross and ascended and he started preaching in Corinth in about 52 A.D. and wrote the letters in about 57. So, we are picking it up in chapter four. The first four chapters are focusing on division and he’s still addressing divisional members of the Corinthian church. He continues to warn them or alert them about their proud, arrogant thinking.

▶ 0:46 Remember, they were saying, “I’m of Paul, I’m of Apollos, I’m of Peter,” and one fourth said, “We’re none of those guys, we’re of Jesus.” One fourth of the people had it right. Okay, three fourths had it wrong. And remember, he got them for quarreling, being splitting and dividing into groups, being jealous of one another, thinking like a natural man instead of a spiritual man, being carnal, being babies and on the milk. After these several years, he said, you’re still on milk. You should be taking meat. And he says, you judge other ministers and other believers that you do not approve of. That’s what we read in the past three chapters.

Why do Christians stop growing?

▶ Watch this part (1:38)

Short answer: He says that when we distract from Jesus Christ and the power of the gospel, “the source of growth,” we stop growing. He adds that pride in our own beliefs and gifts crowds out the One who gave them: “if you receive them, what do you got to brag about?”

Transcript of this part

▶ 1:38 And with motives that elevate your own pride in your beliefs, in your gifts that you receive from Christ. And remember, he said, if you receive them, what do you got to brag about? It’s the one that gave you these things that should be the one you’re praising. Remember all that? And he said, you’re distracting from Jesus Christ and the power of the gospel that gives you growth. And when we distract from the source of growth, how many of you know we stopped growing? Yeah. It’s not the church group. It’s not the minister. It’s Jesus, his gospel and the cross. That’s the power of God that changes our life causes us to grow.

▶ Watch this part (2:31)

Short answer: He says Paul is comparing the Corinthians’ opinion of themselves with how real ministers of God lived. In their own minds they were strong and full and had authority, and Paul says he wishes they did, “so we could have that authority with you to rule as kings.”

So he says, you are full, you are rich, you reign as kings without us. And I would to God that you did reign, that we might reign with you. He says, in your mind, you are strong, you are full, you know, you don’t have any need. You have authority. He said, I wish you did have that authority. So we could have that authority with you to rule as kings.

Who is greatest in God’s kingdom?

▶ Watch this part (3:32)

Paul says the apostles were put last, “a spectacle” to the whole world, while the Corinthians thought they had it all together.

And the recognition works different in the kingdom. He says, us apostles are last. We’re appointed to death, ruin, for we are made a spectacle, a theater to the whole world, to the angels and to men. He says, but not you guys. You think you are full, you know, of food, you don’t need anything materially, you don’t need anything spiritually, you know, you’re rich, you don’t need anything materially, you don’t need anything spiritually. You guys in your mind, you got it together and us apostles are all wrong or our ministers are all wrong.

How should you answer an insult?

▶ Watch this part (11:07)

Short answer: He says Paul wrote “we are reviled,” and when we’re spoken evil of, “we give good words back.” He calls that the sign of a real believer: when verbally abused, they speak back kindness and goodness. When mistreated, he adds, “we permit it” and “don’t retaliate back.”

Transcript of this part

▶ 11:07 He says, we are reviled. We’re spoken evil of. And when we’re spoken evil of, we give good words back. Wow. Did you hear that? He says, that’s the sign of a real minister. That’s the sign of a real believer that’s thinking right. Even when they’re spoken evil of and verbally abused, they speak back kindness and goodness.

▶ 11:46 Now I don’t know about you, but this is good stuff. See, this is the power that Paul is talking about, that God used to build the Corinthian church, the power of a moral, living, moral standard lifestyle of living and possessing the characteristics of God. Powerful. He says, we’re persecuted. We’re mistreated. And we permit it. Suffer means we permit it. We allow it. We just keep preaching to them. We just keep speaking the gospel. You know, we don’t retaliate back.

What makes a real father?

▶ Watch this part (15:01)

Short answer: He says Paul told the Corinthians they had “10,000 tutors in Christ” who just talk, but not many fathers. A father gives life and is also “a parental guide to bring that child up to maturity,” setting aside the time and labor to raise a child through every stage.

You got a lot of these kind of people talking, but you don’t have many fathers.

▶ 15:45 What’s a father? A father is the one that gives life. To a child without the man, the woman’s worthless. Can the gospel really give you new life?

▶ Watch this part (17:40)

Short answer: He says Paul wrote “I have begotten you through the gospel.” Fathers give birth, and “the gospel births.” His own opinions don’t bring life, he says, but if you receive the gospel and embrace it, it gives you “life that keeps growing.”

Transcript of this part

▶ 17:40 Paul’s good, folks. I’ve told you for 40 years, you can’t beat Peter, John and Paul as authors. There is no book that supersedes them. And these other New Testament authors. “I have begotten you through the gospel.” I’ve birthed you. See, Father’s birth, the gospel births. See, my opinions don’t birth. They don’t bring life to you. But the gospel births you. If you will receive it and embrace it, it will give you life that keeps growing.

What does it mean for Christ to be formed in you?

▶ Watch this part (20:55)

Short answer: He says the objective of the gospel is “to form Christ in you so that you are a light to the world.” The goal isn’t just to be a believer or a disciple, but to let your faith and discipline bring you to where Christ is formed in you. As a young Christian struggling to live for Christ, he says, that gave him “tremendous hope.”

Transcript of this part

▶ 20:55 And look at this. “My little children,” Galatians 4:19, “of whom I travail in birth again until Christ be formed in you.” So what’s the objective of the gospel? To form Christ in you so that you are a light to the world. A city set on a hill that everybody can see that imitates Jesus Christ.

▶ 21:36 See, the goal isn’t to be a believer. The goal isn’t to be a disciple. The goal is to let your faith and your discipline in Christ bring you to where Christ is formed in you. I don’t know about you, but when I read that verse as a young Christian man, that just really gave me tremendous hope because I was struggling living for Christ. And then that Holy Ghost came along. Wow, that was a big boost in my ability to say no to sin and yes to right. And then these scriptures, God is in me and he’s shaping me into himself, so I could accept where I was at while I was pursuing the goal.

He says it does: he can’t flatter his kids, and in the natural it takes discipline, responsibility, education and job training. In the spirit world it is the same, he says. It takes giving time to God in prayer and in the Word, and changing your life to represent what you read. “It takes work.”

Why does he say no book supersedes Peter, John and Paul?

He says he has told the church for 40 years that you can’t beat Peter, John and Paul as authors. “There is no book that supersedes them.”

Is the goal of church to make people feel good?

He says that is not God’s concern: “It’s got nothing to do with feeling good and being happy. It’s got to do with growing.” He says the gospel, the cross and Jesus are what cause us to grow.

What does Paul mean by “be followers of me”?

He says Paul told them not to follow one another or follow these little groups, but to follow him: “Follow me because I’m following Christ.” Paul is a real minister, he says, who lives and speaks what he teaches.
