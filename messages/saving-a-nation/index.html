Saving a Nation: full transcript and answers

Single message · Full sermon · Pastor David G. Kincer, Vine Cluster Church · Uploaded June 18, 2014 · 32:52

Watch the full message on YouTube

In “Saving a Nation,” Pastor David G. Kincer says the whole Bible, Old Testament included, was written for our learning, and uses Judges 17–18 (Micah, the tribe of Dan and the people of Laish) to picture a land where everyone does what is right in his own eyes. He says the church must stop living careless and self-confident, speak the Word of God in public, and unite over Bible morality. He also speaks about politics, entitlements, scientism and several controversial moral issues, and says the turning of a nation starts with the church.

Is the Old Testament still for us today?

▶ Watch this part (0:00)

Short answer: He says everything written in the Bible was written for us to learn by, so the Old Testament Scriptures are very important to us. When God dealt with a nation, or a nation fell, we need to learn why and how to turn it around. He says the Old Testament events “occurred” and were not myths, and that “All Scripture” is profitable, with righteousness and good works as the focus of the Bible.

Transcript of this part

▶ 0:00 Everything written in the Bible was written for us to learn by. So Old Testament Scriptures are very important to us. All the Scriptures are important to us. And to remind you of this, Romans 15:4 says, “Whatsoever things were written aforetime were written for our learning, that we, through patience and comfort of the Scriptures, might have hope.” So everything in the Bible is for us to go back and learn by. When God dealt with a nation, or a nation fell, or whatever, we need to learn why they did that and how to turn it around.

▶ 0:45 1 Corinthians 10:11 says, “All these things happened.” Now, not like Bill O’Reilly on Fox News, who says these were allegories, fictitious stories, myths, okay? And we know that the end of the world started when Jesus died on the cross and ascended back into heaven and started his church. We’re in the last days of God’s calendar. We don’t know how many years that’s going to be, but we’re in the ends, and this is for our learning. You got it?

Where did government entitlements come from?

▶ Watch this part (6:13)

Short answer: He says entitlements started when Roosevelt began the New Deal during the Depression, when families could no longer take care of families. The problem, he says, was that it never got removed. It kept growing and came to be expected as a right, and it robbed families of being self-supporting. He adds that in the Bible the family is still a high priority and is held accountable to take care of its members.

Transcript of this part

▶ 6:13 Entitlements, we told you, started when Roosevelt started the New Deal. And Roosevelt himself said this: up to this time the family was a firm, like a law firm, a self-supporting group. The family took care of family. But see, when this Depression came along — I showed you pictures — people were out of work. Families couldn’t take care of families anymore. Many families could not take care of their families anymore. And Roosevelt came in with the New Deal, and he gave stimulus to people, gave aid to people, and so forth. And the poor people, of course, they turned Democratic and voted that way because they needed to survive.

▶ 7:09 Amen. The problem was it never got removed. It kept growing, even when our economy was great. And these entitlements were expected. It was a right: “It’s my right as an American to get these things.” And then it robbed the families of being a self-supporting entity for each family member’s success. See, and folks, in the Bible the family is still a high priority, and they are held accountable to take care of family members in the Bible.

What does it mean to live “careless” and “secure” in your own feelings?

▶ Watch this part (14:57)

Short answer: He says the people of Laish “dwelt careless,” which in the Hebrew means they felt secure, going by their feelings, not by Scripture or the Lord. He calls it the libertarian mentality: “don’t get involved, don’t stand up for anything.” He says the word “secure” means self-confidence, like football players pumping themselves up, and he asks, “Sound like the American church?”

Transcript of this part

▶ 14:57 So they picked up this guy’s gods and this guy’s Levite, and they went up to Laish, and they found some people that had this kind of attitude, this kind of lifestyle. The people of Laish dwelt careless. All right, “careless” there in the Hebrew is they felt secure — their feelings.

▶ 15:34 See, when I feel this is okay, I feel I’m going to heaven — sounds like America. Yeah. Going by their feelings, not by Scripture, not by the Lord. They dwelt careless; they dwelt quietly. They were not involved. They’re really libertarians. You know what a libertarian is, don’t you? Yeah. As long as I’m not hurting anybody, I have the freedom to do what I want to do. That’s the libertarian mentality: don’t get involved, don’t stand up for anything. I am an individual, and whatever you want to believe, you believe. And so this is their attitude.

▶ 16:22 All right, they were secure. Now, “secure” and “careless” are very close words, very close in the Hebrew. But this word “secure,” unlike “careless,” they felt secure; this word “secure” is they had self-confidence. Now, you ever seen these football players, before they go to play a game? They get in a huddle and they’re all pumped up. “Man, oh yeah, we’re going to win, we’re going to win, we’re going to win, we’re going to win! If we say it, we’ll get it. If we claim it, we’ll get it. Oh yeah, yeah!” And they hype themselves up psychologically, and they go in there feeling self-confident, and they lose. Somebody’s got to lose. Both teams are doing it, and one of them can’t come off a winner. Sound like the American church? Absolutely. They got personal self-confidence; they psych themselves up.

▶ 17:20 Just like Sidon dwelt — lived this whole Phoenician thing — so they were connected to this Phoenician Empire, but they were on the border, and there was no magistrate, no judge, no government to shame them in anything. You see, this is the libertarian mentality: there’s no central government to deal with anything, everybody was independently, individually doing what they wanted to do.
