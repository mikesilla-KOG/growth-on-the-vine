How can a human fit the “mark of righteousness” when no one is righteous? (Genesis 12:2-3)

▶ Watch this part (0:00)

Short answer: He says the Bible “requires righteousness to inherit his promises,” yet “there’s none righteous, no, not one.” To find out how, he looks at Abraham, who was promised “a great nation,” a great name and protection, and that through his seed “all the families of the earth” would be blessed.

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 0:00 From creation to the book of Revelation, the Bible requires righteousness to inherit his promises, his benefits of life eternal, health, the necessity of necessities of life, the fruit of the Spirit like joy, peace, longsuffering and so forth, and all the rest. Remember when they came out of Egypt, God promised the blessings in the city, the blessings in the field, the blessings on the offspring, your children’s children. They said none would be barren among you. “I will bless you with food, I’ll bless you with houses, and I’ll bless you by having none of these diseases of Egypt to come upon you, if you obey my word and do right,” fitting into that mark of righteousness. But how can a human fit into that righteous goal and that righteous requirement when the Bible says there’s none righteous, no, not one, and all of sin to come short of the glory of God?

▶ 1:05 To find out how we can, let’s take a look at a man that obtained the goal, and that is Abraham. God came to Abraham in Genesis 12:2 and said, “I’m going to make you a great nation. I am going to bless you. I’m going to make your name great. I’m going to make you a blessing to other people. I’ll bless those that bless you and curse those who curse you.” I’m going to protect you from those that hate you and despise you and want to misuse you. “I’m going to protect you, and you shall all the families of the earth be blessed.” In other words, Jesus Christ is going to come through your seed, and Jesus did, he fulfilled that by coming and turning people from sin to righteousness. In Genesis 13:16 he said, “I will make your seed as the dust of the earth.”

What failures did Abraham have, and was he still counted righteous?

▶ Watch this part (2:57)

Short answer: He lists Abraham’s failures: lying about Sarah “out of fear for his own life,” running from the land during famine, and having a child by Sarah’s maid, which led to “a broken home.” He says Abraham even “laughed at God,” “and yet Abraham received every one of these promises because he believed God, and it was counted to him for righteousness.”

Abraham sees himself as old, ready to be put on the shelf, ready for retirement. His wife is old besides. He sees his failures in some areas to do right. He had lied twice about Sarah being his sister, not his wife, out of fear for his own life. He put Sarah on the line twice.
