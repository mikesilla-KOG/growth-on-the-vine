Did Jesus turn the other cheek when the synagogue tried to throw him off a cliff?

▶ Watch this part (0:36)

Short answer: He reads Luke 4: that Jesus spoke truth, they were angry unlike psychological self-esteem preachers, dragged him to throw him over a cliff—and he did not turn the cheek; passing through their midst he went his way with supernatural power, without blessing or praying for them, because they were criminals wanting to kill him.

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 0:36 And we’re going to talk about adding love to our faith. We’re going to take a look at how Jesus applied these statements of loving your enemy. Did Jesus always turn the other cheek? Luke 4:28, Jesus went to the synagogue and he preached and spoke things there, and when the people in the synagogue heard what he had to say, they weren’t very happy. They were angry. They were mad. Not like your psychological self-esteem preachers of today in America. He said the truth and the truth made them mad. And they dragged him out of the city, took him to the peak of the hill to throw him over the cliff. Now here he is—they’re mad, they’re going to throw him over the cliff. Now here’s Jesus—his chance to turn the other cheek. Go, yeah, go ahead and throw me.

▶ 1:43 He didn’t. He, passing through the midst of them, went his way. He flexed his muscles, his supernatural power, then just walked right through them. He didn’t turn the other cheek. Because these were criminals. They wanted to kill him. If we gave in to every criminal, nobody would be alive, and you wouldn’t have anything. John 8:59, they took up stones to stone him.
