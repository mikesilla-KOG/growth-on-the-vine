🎙From the sermonIt’s a Miracle · Pastor David G. Kincer▶ 0:00

In this message Pastor David G. Kincer defines a miracle as something only God can do and gives two purposes for miracles in Scripture: to get people’s attention and to meet needs. He explains why some refuse to believe even after seeing one, shows from John the Baptist that miracles are not required to believe, and calls repentance and the new birth the greatest miracle. He then teaches the story of the woman caught in adultery in John 8, and closes with a trust demonstration on faith and a call to believe for miracles in marriage and in salvation.

What is a miracle, and how does he describe the parting of the Red Sea? (Exodus 14:21-22)

▶ Watch this part (0:00)

Short answer: He defines a miracle as “something beyond mankind’s ability to do. Human beings can’t do it, only God can do it.” He says it is not “the miracle of science. That’s not a miracle if mankind can do it.” He argues that “an army will not drown in one foot of water,” and that “God miraculously made the bottom of that sea dry.”

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 0:00 A miracle is something beyond mankind’s ability to do. Human beings can’t do it, only God can do it. It’s called a miracle. We’re not talking about the miracle of science. That’s not a miracle if mankind can do it. That’s not a miracle. A miracle is something only God can do. And just for starters, these scientists say that the day the Israelites crossed over, it was at a place where there was one foot of water. Anybody with any brains between their ears knows that an army will not drown in one foot of water. God parted the Red Sea.

▶ 0:46 The sides of the water congealed, they coagulated like buttermilk, only thicker, and they stabilized, and Israel went across on dry land. It didn’t take any time. God miraculously made the bottom of that sea dry. It takes days for a lake to become dry after the water’s gone. And then God destroyed the Egyptian army in that sea, because He miraculously, supernaturally, beyond mankind’s capability, parted those waters. Hallelujah. Same with creation. Scientists can say all they want, but when God spoke and said, “Let there be light,” there was instant light.

Why does he say creation was instant, and what are two purposes of miracles? (Genesis 1:3; John 2:23; John 6:2)

▶ Watch this part (1:35)
