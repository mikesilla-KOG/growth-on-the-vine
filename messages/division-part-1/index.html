Why do young people leave the church?

▶ Watch this part (0:00)

Short answer: Pastor Kincer says the claim that 75% of young people are out of the church after high school is not new: it has been there since he became a Christian, and it used to be higher. When he stayed away as a young person, he says, it was his own decision, not the church’s fault. He wanted to go his own way and sin.

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 0:00 There’s a new book out called You Lost Us. It’s going to sell millions of copies, like the other ones did that I’ve brought out before. And it’s all about attacking the church, blaming the church for the falling away of young people — Barna [unclear], and all these people, you know — and I debated with them this week, because I think they’re wrong.

▶ 0:40 Number one, the statistic that 75% of young people are out of the church when they leave high school and go into college has always been there since I’ve been a Christian. In fact, it was a little higher. If it’s only 75%, we gained some ground. It used to be 80-some percent. Okay, that’s not a new statistic. Back when I was a young person, I didn’t go to church because I wanted to do my thing. It wasn’t the church’s fault. It was my decision. I wanted to sin. I wanted to go the way I wanted to go. I didn’t want any church or any God bridling me and telling me what to do. Don’t tell me what I can and can’t do.

Should we blame the church, or take responsibility?

▶ Watch this part (1:31)

Short answer: He says God told him, “Son, don’t complain of the church. Get in and make a difference in the church.” It is easy to blame a church, he says, but another thing to take it on your own shoulders to find your God and make a difference. He adds that being baptized in the Holy Ghost brings power, the supernatural ability to make good, right, wise decisions.

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 1:31 When I got saved and I complained to God about these things, he told me, “Son, don’t complain of the church. Get in and make a difference in the church.” See, when you’re baptized in the Holy Ghost, you can actually get direction before you ever know the Scriptures, because the Holy Spirit will always give you the nature of God and the truth of the Scriptures until you know them. Do you understand that? And the baptism of the Holy Ghost is very important. Remember, he said you’d receive power. He didn’t say you’d receive tongues and prophecy. He said you would receive what? Power — supernatural ability to make good decisions, right decisions, wise decisions. Are you with me so far?

▶ 2:28 And it’s easy to blame the Catholic church, the Baptist church, the Pentecostal church. It’s easy to blame. It’s another thing to take it on your shoulders to be responsible to find your God, and take it on your shoulders that I can make the difference in the church.

Does the church reject science?

▶ Watch this part (2:53)

Short answer: He calls that claim a lie. He says that in this generation there are movies, DVDs, pamphlets and books on science and the Bible, so no young person has an excuse for not understanding, though he adds that the church cannot talk about science in the Bible every Sunday.

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 2:53 So they said young people — after they were interviewed in the statistics, and they went all through asking young people why they left the church — one of them was science. The church does not embrace science. I said, that’s a lie. We don’t go around talking about science all the time, but in this generation, like no generation before, there is such materials about science in the Bible that no young person has any excuse for not understanding. Could that be true? You can get movies, you can get DVDs, you can get pamphlets, you can get books on the issue and read them yourself. But the church can’t get up every Sunday and talk about science in the Bible.

Is church boring?

▶ Watch this part (3:42)

Short answer: He says church felt boring to him at 16 to 18 because he would not get involved. He was a clock-watcher who did not want to sing to the Lord or pray. David said he was glad to go to the house of the Lord (Psalm 122:1), and he says anyone who does not want to get involved in church, or in God, will be bored and will find every excuse not to be involved.

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 3:42 Anyway, the second thing is the young people said church was boring. Well, that’s exactly what I said when I was 17, 16, 18: church is boring — because I didn’t get involved. I wanted to do my thing. I didn’t want to sing unto the Lord. I didn’t want to get down and pray. I just couldn’t wait for the thing to get over. I was the clock watcher. When is this people going to quit? In fact, if I could, I’d get there late so I didn’t have to endure. The only thing is, my dad was never late to anything, and I picked up the habit. But a lot of us were late because we don’t want to spend any more minutes in that boring place than we have to, or we just lost our desire for Jesus.

▶ 4:40 David said, “I was glad when they said unto me, Let us” — what? — “go to the house of the Lord.” Man, he was excited. It’s assembly time. Oh, glory, let’s go! And, see, we’ve lost all that. A lot of Christians have lost all that, and so they write these books blaming the church. But the church is made up of who? People. And it’s the people that are the determination of a church. Hallelujah. And so I go, that’s not nothing new. Anybody that doesn’t want to get involved in the church, involved in God, are going to be bored. Hallelujah. They’ll find every excuse not to be involved. Church, we can blame till we’re blue in the face, but the thing is, it’s our problem. It’s your problem. It’s your decision. Hallelujah.

Can a divided home stand?

▶ Watch this part (12:44)

Short answer: He says no: husband and wife need to stand together in the same truth and the same moral standards, and show their children Jesus in their lives, or the home will not stand. When parents go two different ways, he says, conflict follows and kids cannot wait to get out of the home.

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 12:44 In the home, is it imperative that the husband and wife stand together with the same truth, the same moral standards? Is it important? And being an example to their children — not just telling their children about Jesus, but showing them in their life. Yes, it is. Why? Or the home isn’t going to stand. See, why do we get divorce in America, even in the Christian church? Because the husband and wife go two different ways. They start getting different beliefs, and then their actions come out of those beliefs, and then there’s conflict in the home. And kids can’t wait to get out of that conflict. They want to get out of home. You know, they’ll do anything. “Send me to college.” “Let me get married.” “I’ll join the military.” “Just get me out of this mess.”

▶ 13:47 The unity is very important. And your kids’ parents need to see you in unity around Jesus, around truth — especially the morals of the Bible. Second Coming doesn’t mean a thing to kids. They need to see our moral standards. Amen? They need to see our tongue speaking rightly, our anger curbed. You shall receive what when the Holy Ghost comes upon you? Power. Hallelujah. So are we offered the sources, the solutions? Absolutely. You can’t violate these things.

How should Christians handle disagreements in the church?

▶ Watch this part (14:39)

Short answer: He says to be joined in the same mind and judgment, and to judge “right judgment” rather than by personal opinion, with decisions backed up by the Word of God, with two or three verses (John 7:24). He also says that when we disagree with something in the church, we should not go tell the whole world, because it weakens the church more.

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 14:39 And so the church should not be like it is today. “Perfectly joined in the same mind and judgment” — completely joined in the same thought, the same understanding. “Judge not according to appearance,” John 7:24, “but judge” what? Right judgment. Don’t judge for your personal opinion. Make decisions that are right and backed up by the Word of God, with two or three verses. “It’s been declared that there is quarreling, arguments, contentions among you.” And that’s what we do. If we disagree with it, we go out and blab to the world. Isn’t that right? We disagree with something going on in the church or some brother or something, we go out and tell everybody in the world. And it just weakens the church more.

Who should we follow — a pastor, a denomination, or Christ?

▶ Watch this part (15:39)

Short answer: Of the four groups in Corinth (“I’m of Paul,” “of Apollos,” “of Peter,” “of Christ”), he says only the Christ group is right. He says our unity comes from Christ, who is one with the Father and the Scriptures, not from a church name, a church group or a minister, and he asks whether the Pope, priests, Pentecostals or Baptists can save anyone. His answer is no.

🎙From the sermonTranscript of this part · Pastor David G. Kincer

▶ 15:39 Okay, here’s the focus of the division. Can you rationally, reasonably, logically tell me which group is right? Yeah, the Christ group. Hallelujah. And he explains it: is Christ divided? See, is Christ one with the Father? Is Christ one with the Scriptures? Is he united with the Father and the Scriptures? Yes. So where do I find my source of unity? In Christ, and me being like him, in unity with God and in unity with the Spirit. Not my church’s opinions, not my opinions, but Christ, in unity with his Father and in unity with the Scriptures.

He sums up Paul’s three statements: follow and imitate Jesus, focus on Jesus, and preach Christ, his gospel and the cross, where the power of God is.
