What are the four stages of a newborn baby in Christ? (1 Peter 2:2-5)

▶ Watch this part (0:00)

Short answer: He says the four baby stages are: desiring the pure milk of the Word, coming to Jesus Christ (not a church, a minister or a group), being built up as a spiritual house and priesthood, and offering spiritual sacrifices. He says that if you are not doing these things “you haven’t been born again,” and you are still in the embryo stage.

▶ 0:00 And we are going to be talking about the newborn baby stage in Christ. There are four baby stages, and they’re not necessarily in order or how they are carried out by God. In 1 Peter 2:2 it says, “As newborn babies,” we’re not in the embryo anymore, we’re not being developed inside, we got an outside birth, “newborn babies.” Number one, the first stage listed here in Peter is “desire the pure,” sincere, “milk of the word,” the genuine milk of the Word, “that you may grow thereby.” And so you don’t want to go to [unclear]’s books, my books, or Robert’s books; you want to go to God’s book. What does he say the embryo stage is?

He says if you are not in the Word and growing, not coming to Jesus, and not letting God build you by the Word and the Spirit, “you’re still in the embryo stage.” In other words, “you haven’t been born again.”

What three baptisms does he list?

He lists baptism in the body of Christ, “where God places in as He sees fit,” baptism in the Holy Ghost, “where the power of God comes upon you to be a witness unto Him,” and baptism in water, “where your old man of sin is put to death and you rise a new creature.”

What does he say repentance looks like for a baby Christian?

He says a baby Christian “is one that’s turned from sin,” stopping being a thief, stopping sexual immorality and stopping getting drunk and high, and turning to right. He says you may still struggle but “you’re turning from them, that’s the baby stage.”

He calls it rebellion. He says when the Bible says to watch one hour, pay your tithe, or assemble together and “we won’t do it, what is it? Rebellion.”

What does he say the goal of assembling together is?

He says it is “his habitation,” God’s presence coming. It is “not to get a lady, not to get a man, not to get someone to buy my product,” but to assemble for His presence to come.
