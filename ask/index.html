Can a Christian have a demon?

Partly covered in the sermons

Answer woven from Scripture (BSB) and the sermons

Thoughtful Christians have understood this question in more than one way, and no single verse answers it in so many words. What Scripture makes clear is that the struggle is real and that Christ is greater. It speaks of “the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly realms” (BSB, Ephesians 6:11-12), and it also says “Resist the devil, and he will flee from you” (BSB, James 4:7) and “greater is He who is in you than he who is in the world” (BSB, 1 John 4:4).

One view: a believer belongs to Christ

Some Christians point to passages about who a believer belongs to. God “has rescued us from the dominion of darkness” (BSB, Colossians 1:13), a believer's body is “a temple of the Holy Spirit” (BSB, 1 Corinthians 6:19-20), and “the evil one cannot touch him” (BSB, 1 John 5:18). Pastor Kincer takes this view in one short remark, right after speaking of the power of the cross, in “Division Concluded Part 2”: “Nobody has more power than Jesus Christ.” He goes on, “That’s why a Christian can’t have a demon and a Christian, you know, is not under the influence of Satan because Jesus is in us.” ▶ Division Concluded Part 2 9:04

Another view: a believer can still be attacked or misled

Others point to warnings that believers can still be attacked or led astray: “Your adversary the devil prowls around like a roaring lion, seeking someone to devour” (BSB, 1 Peter 5:8); “do not give the devil a foothold” (BSB, Ephesians 4:26-27); “your minds may be led astray from your simple and pure devotion to Christ” (BSB, 2 Corinthians 11:3). Peter's question to Ananias is also cited: “how is it that Satan has filled your heart” (BSB, Acts 5:3). Christians read that verse in different ways. We did not find Pastor Kincer teaching this second view in the six messages on our site, so we are not putting it in his mouth.

Is church boring?

Covered in the sermons

Answer woven from Scripture (BSB) and the sermons

Lots of people feel this way, and it is fair to ask. Pastor Kincer says he felt the same as a teenager. In “Division Part 1” he says “church is boring — because I didn’t get involved”, and describes himself as “the clock watcher” ▶ Division Part 1 3:42.

He goes on: “Anybody that doesn’t want to get involved in the church, involved in God, are going to be bored.” ▶ Division Part 1 4:40 In “A More Excellent Way” he says “when people start taking responsibility to be involved in a church service, you will be shocked at what God can do” ▶ A More Excellent Way 22:20, and in “Division Concluded Part 2” that “the power of one member just being present, God uses to touch people’s lives” ▶ Division Concluded Part 2 17:41.

We are told, “let us encourage one another” (BSB, Hebrews 10:24-25), and Paul describes a gathering where “everyone has a psalm or a teaching, a revelation, a tongue, or an interpretation” (BSB, 1 Corinthians 14:26).

What does the Bible say about love?

Covered in the sermons

Answer woven from Scripture (BSB) and the sermons

Love sits at the center of the Bible, and it starts with God. “God so loved the world that He gave His one and only Son” (BSB, John 3:16). “While we were still sinners, Christ died for us” (BSB, Romans 5:8), and “We love because He first loved us” (BSB, 1 John 4:19).

Jesus called loving God “the first and greatest commandment” (BSB, Matthew 22:37-39), with loving your neighbor as the second like it. He said, “By this everyone will know that you are My disciples, if you love one another” (BSB, John 13:34-35). John writes, “Whoever does not love does not know God, because God is love” (BSB, 1 John 4:7-8).

Paul says that without love even great gifts are “a ringing gong or a clanging cymbal” (BSB, 1 Corinthians 13:1-3). He describes love this way: “Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud” (BSB, 1 Corinthians 13:4-7). And “the greatest of these is love” (BSB, 1 Corinthians 13:13).

Pastor Kincer has a whole message on this, “A More Excellent Way”. Walking through 1 Corinthians 13, he says “love doesn’t demand its own way, in the family, in the church, or in the community. It demands the right way, the truth.” ▶ A More Excellent Way 6:24 Reading Paul's words to Timothy, he says “the goal of the word of God is love out of a pure heart”, which matches “The goal of our instruction is the love that comes from a pure heart, a clear conscience, and a sincere faith” (BSB, 1 Timothy 1:5) ▶ A More Excellent Way 23:07. He adds, “love is at the peak of divine character, and it’s at the beginning of the fruits of the Spirit” ▶ A More Excellent Way 8:22. In “Division Part 2” he says “love is not only spelled L-O-V-E, it’s spelled T-I-M-E” ▶ Division Part 2 21:18.

Do I have to speak in tongues?

Covered in the sermons

Answer woven from Scripture (BSB) and the sermons

This is a gentle question with a long history. We did not find a place in the six messages where Pastor Kincer says you must speak in tongues to be saved. Scripture ties salvation to something else: “if you confess with your mouth, ‘Jesus is Lord,’ and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved” (BSB, Romans 10:9-10), and “it is by grace you have been saved through faith” (BSB, Ephesians 2:8-9).

The Bible does describe tongues as a gift of the Spirit. One gift is “speaking in various tongues” (BSB, 1 Corinthians 12:7-11), and the Spirit “apportions them to each one as He determines” (BSB, 1 Corinthians 12:7-11). Paul asks, “Do all speak in tongues?” (BSB, 1 Corinthians 12:29-30) and writes, “I wish that all of you could speak in tongues, but I would rather have you prophesy” (BSB, 1 Corinthians 14:1-5). In Acts, believers were “filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other tongues” (BSB, Acts 2:4), and “they spoke in tongues and prophesied” (BSB, Acts 19:6). Jesus said the Father will “give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him” (BSB, Luke 11:13).

Two things Pastor Kincer holds together

He recommends being filled with the Holy Spirit and speaks in tongues himself. In “Division Part 2” he says “I highly recommend the baptism of the Holy Ghost” ▶ Division Part 2 6:36, and of his own experience, “I was filled with the Holy Ghost with speaking in tongues” ▶ Division Part 2 5:50. But he also says it is not the goal. In “A More Excellent Way”: “That’s not the goal of the commandment. That’s one of the attributes that gives you power to live for God.” He says “God is the one that distributes these things as he wills” ▶ A More Excellent Way 24:24, and that “love is more important than both of those” ▶ A More Excellent Way 0:00. Of a Baptist pastor who does not share his view on tongues, he says “I would unite with this guy any day on Bible morals” ▶ Saving a Nation 26:56.

A view he disagrees with

Some Christians teach that tongues have ceased, pointing to Paul's words that “where there are tongues, they will be restrained” (BSB, 1 Corinthians 13:8-10) (other translations say they will cease). Pastor Kincer tells of Baptist leaders who said this and says he answered, “Knowledge is there. And it says knowledge ceased.” ▶ A More Excellent Way 11:10

Why does the church have divisions?

Covered in the sermons

Answer woven from Scripture (BSB) and the sermons

Divisions are not new. Paul wrote to the church in Corinth, “there are quarrels among you” (BSB, 1 Corinthians 1:11-13): people were saying, “‘I follow Paul,’ ‘I follow Apollos,’ ‘I follow Cephas,’ or ‘I follow Christ.’” (BSB, 1 Corinthians 1:11-13) He asked, “Is Christ divided?” (BSB, 1 Corinthians 1:11-13) and pleaded “that all of you agree together, so that there may be no divisions among you” (BSB, 1 Corinthians 1:10). He named the cause: “there is jealousy and dissension among you” (BSB, 1 Corinthians 3:3-4). James says the same: “where jealousy and selfish ambition exist, there will be disorder and every evil practice” (BSB, James 3:16).

Pastor Kincer, in his Corinthians series, points to pride and to following people instead of Jesus. In “Division Concluded Part 1” he recalls the Corinthians saying “I’m of Paul, I’m of Apollos, I’m of Peter” ▶ Division Concluded Part 1 0:46, and says “It’s not the church group. It’s not the minister. It’s Jesus, his gospel and the cross.” ▶ Division Concluded Part 1 1:38 In “Division Concluded Part 2”: “It’s your own prideful thinking that makes you want to pick and choose.” ▶ Division Concluded Part 2 4:53 He sums it up, “He has showed us what causes it. He has showed us what cures it. And it starts with us.” ▶ Division Concluded Part 2 21:33

Both sides, one: stay together around Jesus

On how to respond, he teaches two things in different messages. First, stay united around Jesus and do not split over details: “Let’s keep the focus on Jesus, the gospel and the cross so people can grow.” ▶ Division Concluded Part 2 11:17 Of a pastor he disagrees with on several things, he says “But I would unite with this guy any day on Bible morals” ▶ Saving a Nation 26:56. Scripture speaks of “diligence to preserve the unity of the Spirit through the bond of peace” (BSB, Ephesians 4:1-6), says “Accept him whose faith is weak, without passing judgment on his opinions” (BSB, Romans 14:1), and records Jesus praying “that all of them may be one” (BSB, John 17:20-21).

Both sides, two: some things are worth a line

Second, there are limits. In “Saving a Nation” he lists serious sins, following the list in 1 Corinthians 5:11, and says “A believer who wants to say it’s okay — we are not to fellowship with them.” while adding “Nothing to do with the second coming, nothing to do with a lot of these things that we divide over, how you’re baptized and all that stuff.” ▶ Saving a Nation 27:34 Scripture people point to here includes “watch out for those who create divisions and obstacles that are contrary to the teaching you have learned. Turn away from them” (BSB, Romans 16:17-18) and “Reject a divisive man after a first and second admonition” (BSB, Titus 3:10-11).

Is Jesus the only way to God?

Covered in the sermons

Answer woven from Scripture (BSB) and the sermons

Jesus said this Himself, and the apostles repeated it. Jesus said, “I am the way and the truth and the life. Why does God allow suffering?

Not covered in the sermons yet

Answer woven from Scripture (BSB) and the sermons

This is one of the hardest questions there is, and we want to be honest: the sermons on our site have not covered it yet, so this answer comes from Scripture only. These passages do not explain every hardship, but they show how God meets people in it.

The Bible says God is close to people who hurt. “The LORD is near to the brokenhearted” (BSB, Psalm 34:18-19). David wrote, “Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for You are with me” (BSB, Psalm 23:4). God is called “the God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our troubles” (BSB, 2 Corinthians 1:3-5), and Jesus “was tempted in every way that we are, yet was without sin” (BSB, Hebrews 4:15), so He understands our weakness.

It also says suffering is not the end of the story. Jesus said, “In the world you will have tribulation. But take courage; I have overcome the world!” (BSB, John 16:33) Paul wrote, “our present sufferings are not comparable to the glory that will be revealed in us” (BSB, Romans 8:18), and Peter, “after you have suffered for a little while, the God of all grace” (BSB, 1 Peter 5:10) will restore you. In the end, “there will be no more death or mourning or crying or pain” (BSB, Revelation 21:4).

And Scripture says God can work in us through hard times: “suffering produces perseverance; perseverance, character; and character, hope” (BSB, Romans 5:3-5), and “the testing of your faith develops perseverance” (BSB, James 1:2-4).
