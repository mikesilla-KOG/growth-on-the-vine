Clip · A More Excellent Way

Does real love insist on its own way?

Love doesn’t say “my way or the highway.” Love … does not seek its own, … thinks no evil.

Key Takeaway

Love … does not seek its own, … thinks no evil.

Key Scripture

1 Corinthians 13:4–7 — Love suffers long and is kind; love does not envy; love does not parade itself, is not puffed up; does not behave rudely, does not seek its own, is not provoked, thinks no evil; does not rejoice in iniquity, but rejoices in the truth; bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things.

Paul doesn’t leave love vague. In 1 Corinthians 13:4–7 he defines it — patient, kind, not proud, not rude — and then the line that lands at home and church:

Love suffers long and is kind; love does not envy; love does not parade itself, is not puffed up; does not behave rudely, does not seek its own, is not provoked, thinks no evil; does not rejoice in iniquity, but rejoices in the truth; bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things. — 1 Corinthians 13:4–7

Love does not demand its own way in the family, the church, or the community. It demands the right way — the truth. And it isn’t touchy; it keeps no ledger of hurts.

Ask yourself: Am I demanding my way — or the right way?

Scripture

1 Corinthians 13:4–7 — Love suffers long and is kind; love does not envy; love does not parade itself, is not puffed up; does not behave rudely, does not seek its own, is not provoked, thinks no evil; does not rejoice in iniquity, but rejoices in the truth; bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things.

Plain language: love won’t bully the room into “my way,” and it won’t keep score of wrongs.
